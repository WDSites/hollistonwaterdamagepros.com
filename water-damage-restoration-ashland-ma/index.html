Ashland neighborhoods and how their water behaves

Ashland was formed in 1846 from parts of Framingham, Holliston and Hopkinton, and it still touches all three. Its geography is shaped by the Sudbury River, Ashland Reservoir, Hopkinton Reservoir, Bracket Reservoir and Waushakum Pond.

The town's August 2025 draft hazard mitigation plan identifies 12 locally known flood sites, among them Megunko Hill near Tilton and Cherry Streets and the Concord Street bridge. If you live near the river or one of the reservoirs, your basement is the most likely place for water to appear.

Ashland at a glance

Water

Ashland's public water comes from five groundwater wells, with an emergency connection to the MWRA through Southborough.

Sewer

About 66 miles of sewer main serve the town, and wastewater travels through Framingham to the MWRA system.

Permits

Inspectional Services at 101 Main Street covers building, plumbing, gas and wiring permits.

Dispatch

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock for Ashland addresses, nights and weekends included.

When water is spreading in Ashland

Most Ashland calls start with an ordinary failure, like a washing machine hose, a dishwasher line or a water heater that gave out. Those are usually clean water jobs if they are caught quickly.

A storm is a different story. The draft plan records 17.7 inches of rain over 19 days in March 2010 and a flash flood event across Middlesex County on June 28, 2020. In a sustained rain event, a sump pump can fail while the house is still dry upstairs, and by the time the water shows, it has already soaked the framing.

Call (508) 446-9244 as soon as you see it. The dispatcher will ask where the water came from and whether it looks clean, cloudy or contaminated.

Houses, ages and what that means for repairs

The draft plan counts 7,495 housing units in town, 79 percent of them single-family, and 8.9 percent built before 1940. That means more than nine in ten Ashland homes were built after 1940, and finished basements are a common feature in that kind of housing.

Finished basements are where we spend a lot of time. Carpet, pad, drywall and baseboards all react differently to clean versus contaminated water, which is why the water category drives the plan. See water damage cleanup for how that sorting works.

Dams, reservoirs and what they mean at home

The draft plan lists six dams in Ashland. Mill Pond Dam on the Sudbury River is town-owned and classified high hazard, and the Hopkinton Reservoir dam, the Hopkinton State Park swimming pool dam and the Ashland Reservoir dam are also high hazard. The plan documents no dam failures.

That is a reassuring record, but it is also a reason to know your flood zone. Look up your street on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, and ask your insurer whether a separate flood policy makes sense.

Sources: Ashland draft Hazard Mitigation Plan (August 2025), Ashland Water and Sewer Department pages, Ashland town profile. Ask Inspectional Services about permit rules.

If water is still spreading in Ashland

Stop the flow

Close the valve at the fixture or the main shutoff near the meter. Every minute adds to the damage.

Protect the power

If water is near outlets or the panel, stay out and call your utility or 911.

Move what matters

Lift furniture, move electronics and paper above the water line.

Call the line

Dial (508) 446-9244 and tell the dispatcher you are in Ashland.

Frequently asked questions

Is emergency water removal available in Ashland?

Yes. Our dispatch number (508) 446-9244 answers every hour of the week. Sump pump failures during long rain events are one of the most common after-hours calls. When a team is free, it heads out the same day.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Ashland?

Ashland uses ZIP 01721 throughout, which covers Ashland Center, the Sudbury River corridor and the neighborhoods around Ashland Reservoir. Dial (508) 446-9244 with your address for a coverage answer.

Does Ashland's municipal water affect restoration?

Not directly. Public water comes from groundwater wells, and a burst pipe inside your house is the same job either way. We recommend shutting off at your main valve.

Can you help with a flooded finished basement?

Yes. We extract the water, remove materials the water category does not allow us to keep, dry the structure and document everything for your insurer.

Where do I ask about permits for repairs in Ashland?

Inspectional Services at 101 Main Street handles building, plumbing, gas and wiring permits. We will tell you if your repair needs one.
