When water is spreading in Milford

Milford sits in Worcester County, while Holliston is in Middlesex, but the same 24/7 line covers both. If you have a burst pipe, a flooded basement or an overflowing toilet in Milford, call (508) 446-9244 and describe what you see.

While you wait, close the shutoff for the source if you can, turn power off to wet rooms only when the panel is dry and easy to reach, and move valuables above the floor. Water that sits on a floor for a day starts to wick into walls.

Streets where Milford water goes first

Milford's 2018 Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness report lists the places residents already know flood. They include Beach and Central Streets, the Godfrey Brook, Hospital Brook and O'Brien Brook corridors, Milford Pond Dam, Depot Street at National Street, the Charles River at Howard Street and the Church Street and Water Street area.

The Charles River Watershed Association adds streets around the Godfrey Brook subwatershed that see repeated flooding, including Salvia Drive, Redwood Drive, Hancock Street, Thayer Street and South Main Street. If your basement is near one of those, a sump pump with a battery backup is worth testing before the next storm.

Not every flooded basement sits near a brook. Heavy rain can overwhelm any storm drain, and the road in front of your house does not need to appear in a report for water to find your foundation.

Milford at a glance

Water

The Milford Water Department runs the public system from its Dilla Street plant. Echo Lake, one of its sources, is actually in Hopkinton.

Sewer

The Milford Sewer Department serves town sewer, and the wastewater plant sits in neighboring Hopedale.

Permits

Building and Inspections at 52 Main Street handles building, plumbing, gas and electrical permits for repairs.

Dispatch

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock, including holidays, for every Milford address.

A town built in granite, shoes and brick

The town's history page describes pink granite quarries employing over 1,000 men by 1900, a boot and shoe industry that began with Ariel Bragg, a Town Hall from 1854 and a Memorial Hall from 1884. That heritage shows up in the buildings.

Downtown Milford has older masonry and multi-unit buildings where a leak upstairs can reach neighbors below. Plaster walls, original wood floors and old supply lines are common. In those buildings we cut and dry carefully and tell you what your landlord, condo association or insurer may need to see.

Ariel Bragg's start in Braggville ties Milford to Holliston, since Braggville is one of the villages on our home turf.

What the records say about Milford floods

Milford's MVP report cites federal disaster declarations affecting Worcester County in 2009, 2010, 2012, 2013 and 2015, and it uses FEMA flood zones A, AE and floodway in its mapping. The town has also used FEMA funding for a culvert on Godfrey Brook.

Use that as a reminder to check your own address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center. Flood damage is usually excluded from standard homeowners policies, so confirm what your policy says.

Sources: Milford MVP planning grant report (state), Milford town history page, CRWA flooding report. Ask Milford Building and Inspections about permit rules.

Milford's water system also supplies wholesale water to parts of Medway, and neighbors in Hopkinton share the Echo Lake source, so crews move easily between the three towns.

If water is still spreading in Milford

Mind the power

Cut power to wet rooms only if the panel is dry. If water is near the panel, call the utility or 911.

Lift what you can
