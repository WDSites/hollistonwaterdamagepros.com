Shower Leak Water Damage Repair in Holliston, MA

A shower that leaks behind the tile rarely shows it right away. Here is how to find the source, what it damages below, and the order the repair should follow.

By the Holliston Water Damage Pros team | Published October 29, 2026

☎ (508) 446-9244

Start with the stain, then work back to the shower

Shower leak water damage repair in Holliston, MA begins with a simple question: where is the water actually coming from? A brown ring on the dining room ceiling or a soft spot in the bathroom floor is the symptom. The leak itself is usually a few feet away and a floor above.

Most shower leaks fall into four groups: failed grout or caulk, a leaking valve or supply fitting behind the wall, a cracked shower pan, or a bad drain connection. Each one leaves a different pattern of damage, and each needs a different fix.

If water is actively dripping through a ceiling right now, close the shower supply, put a bucket under the drip and call our crews. Our emergency water removal team can start extraction before the rebuild is ever discussed.

Four common sources, and how each one shows itself

Grout and caulk fail slowly. Hairline gaps at the tub line, a loose corner tile or a hollow sound when you tap the wall all point to water getting behind the surface a little at a time.

Where a shower leak usually starts and what it leaves behind
SourceWhat you noticeWhere damage tends to spread
Grout or caulk gapLoose tile, dark grout lines, soft drywall at the tub edgeWall board behind the tile and the wall base
Valve or supply fittingStain on the wall or ceiling that appears even when nobody showersWall cavity, insulation and the room below
Shower pan or linerDamp curb, musty smell, water marks under the bathroomSubfloor and ceiling beneath the shower
Drain connectionSlow drip under the shower stall that gets worse with each useFloor framing and the ceiling directly below

A leak that shows up only while the shower runs points to the drain, the pan or the wall surface. A leak that shows up with the shower off points to a pressurized line, and that one is more urgent.

Simple tests that narrow it down

You can learn a lot in an hour without cutting anything open. Run the shower for ten minutes and watch the ceiling or access panel below. Then plug the drain, let the pan hold an inch of water for thirty minutes and check again.

If the leak appears only with the pan filled, the drain seal or liner is the likely culprit. If it appears only when water hits the wall, look at grout, caulk and the valve trim. If it appears with everything off, the problem is in a supply line.

  • Take photos of every stain with a coin or tape measure beside it for scale
  • Mark the edge of each stain in pencil so you can see if it grows
  • Check the water meter with every fixture off to see whether it still moves
  • Press gently on tile, drywall and the floor near the stall to find soft spots
  • Look in the room below for any stain, bubble in the paint or sag in the ceiling

A plumber confirms the source and makes the plumbing repair. Restoration does not replace plumbing, and we will tell you plainly if a plumber needs to come first.

What the water damages below the shower

Water from a shower leak follows gravity and framing. It runs along the back of the tile board, down through the sill plate and into the subfloor, then soaks the insulation in the ceiling cavity underneath. The visible stain on the lower floor is often the last thing to show.

Tile board and drywall fail first. They soften, crumble at the edges and grow mold on the paper facing if they stay wet for days. Subfloor plywood swells and delaminates, and the floor around the shower begins to feel springy underfoot.

Wet insulation is the quiet problem. It holds water against the joists and keeps the framing damp long after the leak is fixed. For ceilings that have already stained, our guide to water damage repair explains what comes out and what can stay.

Drying before rebuilding

The state's homeowner guide sets a target of one day for drying wet areas where possible, and the Housing Code allows two days for leaks and floods. A slow shower leak does not follow that clock, but the principle holds. Moisture left in framing is the root of nearly every callback after a repair.

Crews take moisture readings in the wall, floor and ceiling, then open what has to be opened. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run until the readings return to normal for that material. A thermal imaging camera helps find wet pockets that readings from the surface miss.

This stage is water mitigation, and it comes before any new board goes up. Patching over a wet cavity sends you back to the same stain within a season.

The repair sequence, step by step

  1. Stop the leak and have the plumbing repaired or the pan, valve or drain replaced
  2. Remove wet tile board, drywall and insulation back to dry, solid material
  3. Dry the framing and subfloor until moisture readings match unaffected areas
  4. Treat the cavity for surface mold if the readings and a visual check call for it
  5. Replace the subfloor section if it is soft, delaminated or stained through
  6. Hang waterproof backer board, then tile, seal and caulk to the manufacturer's directions
  7. Repair the ceiling below, then prime and paint the stain block to finish

The order matters because every step depends on the one before it. New tile over a damp backer board fails early, and ceiling paint over wet drywall bubbles. Scope and price are explained before work starts so you can see which steps your house actually needs.

Who handles what: plumber, tile setter and restoration crew

A shower leak repair usually involves three trades. The plumber fixes the valve, drain or supply line. The restoration crew removes and dries wet material and rebuilds ceilings and walls. A tile setter or general contractor handles the new shower surface.

Plumbing and wiring work may need a permit, and the Holliston Building Department handles plumbing, gas, wiring and building permits and inspections. Ask the department before work begins instead of guessing what applies to your job.

Crews also work in nearby towns such as Milford and Ashland, and the same sequence applies in older bathrooms there too. No one can promise how an insurer will treat a particular claim, so check your policy and ask your insurer early.

What Holliston's water records say about shower stains

Holliston public water comes from five groundwater supply wells, and iron and manganese filtration operates at three of them. If you see orange or dark brown marks on tile, fixtures or the tub, that is a mineral staining question, not a leak question.

Staining on fixtures is a good reason to call the Holliston DPW Water Division and ask about your own supply. A ceiling stain that grows after a shower is a different matter, and that one is about moisture, not minerals.

Sources: Holliston Water Division water quality report (2019). Ask the Holliston Building Department about permit rules for plumbing work.

When to call, and what to have ready

Call as soon as you see a ceiling stain, a soft floor near the shower or a musty smell that returns after cleaning. Early calls are usually smaller jobs. A leak that runs for months turns a tile repair into a subfloor and framing repair.

Have a short list ready: when you first noticed the stain, whether it changes when the shower runs, and any photos you took. Our dispatch line can describe the next steps, and same-day response is available when crews can be reached.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if my shower is leaking behind the wall?

Loose or hollow-sounding tile, discolored grout, a musty smell and a stain on the ceiling below are the usual signs. A moisture meter gives a reliable answer without opening the wall.

Can I just re-caulk and re-grout the shower?

If the gaps are the only problem and the wall behind is dry, yes. If the board is soft or the readings are high, the water is already behind the surface and caulk only hides it.

How long does the subfloor need to dry?

There is no fixed number of days. Crews dry until readings match normal for that material. Plywood that has delaminated or stained through is usually replaced instead of dried.

Does restoration include fixing the leak itself?

No. A licensed plumber repairs the plumbing, and restoration removes and dries the wet material and rebuilds the damage. We coordinate the order with them.

Will insurance pay for shower leak damage?

Sudden and accidental leaks are often covered, and slow leaks from long-term neglect often are not. Check your policy and ask your insurer, because no one outside your insurer can promise an outcome.

Does Holliston's well water cause shower leaks?

No. Holliston's public water comes from groundwater wells with iron and manganese filtration at three of them, which can affect staining on fixtures. Leaks come from failed seals, fittings and drains, so staining questions go to the Holliston DPW Water Division.
