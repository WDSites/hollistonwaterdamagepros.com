Does House Insurance Cover Water Damage in Holliston, MA?

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while gradual leaks and flooding often are not. Here is how to read your policy and document a loss.

By the Holliston Water Damage Pros team | Published October 29, 2026

☎ (508) 446-9244

The short answer for Holliston homeowners

Does house insurance cover water damage in Holliston, MA? Often yes if the water damage was sudden and accidental, such as a pipe that burst or a washing machine hose that let go. Often no if the damage came from a slow leak, poor upkeep, or flooding from outside the house.

Every policy word differently, so this is general information, not a coverage opinion. Your policy and your insurer have the final word on what is covered.

The rest of this guide explains the split between covered and excluded water and the flood and sewer backup gap. It also covers what to document before the first call. Read it alongside your own policy.

Sudden and accidental versus gradual: the line insurers draw

Most homeowners policies are written around the idea of a sudden, unexpected event. A frozen pipe that splits overnight fits that idea. A supply line that dripped for six months behind a vanity usually does not.

That difference is why speed matters. Water that sat in a wall for weeks looks like neglect on paper, even if you never noticed it. Prompt discovery, prompt drying and prompt reporting all help show the loss was sudden.

Flood damage and the sump pump gap

Flood damage is excluded from standard homeowners and commercial policies in Massachusetts, the Division of Insurance notes. A sump pump or water backup endorsement may provide some coverage, but it is an add-on you have to buy ahead of time.

The difference between a flood and a backup matters. Rain overflowing a brook and entering a basement is flood. Water pushed back up through a floor drain, or a pump that stops working, falls under the backup question, and the endorsement usually carries a limit worth reading.

Coverage is one question and cleanup is another. Our flood damage restoration page explains the stages a crew works through, whatever your coverage turns out to be.

What to do in the first hour of a covered-looking loss

Stop the source if you can. Shut the main water valve for a pipe failure, move to higher ground for a flood, and stay out of rooms with water near electrical outlets.

Then protect the property from further damage. Insurers generally expect reasonable steps to limit the loss, so pulling up soaked rugs and starting extraction is normal. Keep damaged items until your adjuster has seen them or approved their disposal.

  • Photograph and video every wet room before anything is moved
  • Write down the date and time you found the water and what you think caused it
  • Keep receipts for emergency repairs and drying work
  • Save samples of ruined flooring or wallboard if the adjuster asks
  • Call your insurer and ask what they need to start the claim
  • Do not throw out damaged personal property without a list or photos

Documentation that makes a claim easier to review

A restoration company does not decide your claim, but it can give you records an adjuster can use. Dated moisture readings show how wet materials were and when they dried. Equipment logs show what ran and for how long.

A written scope lists what was removed and what remains. Photos taken before demolition show the extent of damage, which can later be compared with the final invoice. Ask any company you hire what its documentation package looks like.

The drying side of that paperwork is covered under water mitigation. Readings are taken and recorded day by day.

Flood insurance and the 30-day waiting period

What flood coverage looks like in Holliston

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says a National Flood Insurance Program policy usually has a 30-day waiting period. That means buying a policy during a storm warning will not help for that storm.

Holliston participates in the NFIP, and the 2024 Hazard Mitigation Plan counted 54 policies in force at the time. Flood plains are mapped along Beaver Brook, Hopping Brook, Lake Winthrop, Chicken Brook and Bogastow Brook. Look up your address at msc.fema.gov and ask your insurance agent whether a separate flood policy fits your situation before the next storm season starts.

Sources: Massachusetts Division of Insurance (mass.gov), Holliston Hazard Mitigation Plan (2024), FEMA Flood Map Service Center.

What a restoration company can and cannot do about coverage

We can dry the structure, document conditions and list what the crew removed. We cannot say what your policy covers, we cannot approve a claim, and no one should promise you a claim outcome. A company that tells you otherwise is guessing.

Scope and price are explained before work starts, and you decide whether to proceed. Some homeowners start mitigation immediately to stop the damage from growing, then handle the paperwork while the equipment runs.

  • Emergency mitigation limits further damage and is usually a reasonable first step
  • Rebuild decisions can wait until the adjuster has visited
  • Ask your insurer which documents they want, not just what you think is useful
  • Keep your own copy of every invoice, moisture log and photo set

Neighbors with different water rules

Water rules vary by property, not just by town. In Sherborn, homes run on private wells and septic systems, so the plumbing and water source differ from a house on public water and sewer. Ask your insurer how your policy treats well and septic equipment.

In Ashland, a draft hazard plan lists 12 locally known flood sites, so some streets have a recorded pattern. Neither fact changes your policy, but both are reasons to read the exclusions before the loss happens.

After the claim: rebuild, repair and your next steps

Once the structure is dry, water damage repair puts the walls, ceilings and floors back together. If your insurer pays for part of it, keep the scope aligned with the adjuster's estimate so there are no surprises on the invoice.

If the claim is denied or only partly paid, ask your insurer to explain the decision in writing, and ask about their review process. Do not hold off on drying while you wait, because the damage keeps growing.

Frequently asked questions

Does homeowners insurance cover a burst pipe?

Often yes, because a burst pipe is a sudden and accidental event. Some policies add conditions, such as maintaining heat in the house or limits when the property is vacant.

Report it right away and start drying so the damage does not spread.

Does insurance cover a slow leak under the sink?

Often not, or only in part. Many policies exclude continuous or repeated seepage over time. The sudden resulting damage may be handled differently from the leak itself.

Your policy language and your insurer decide, so ask for the decision in writing.

Is flood damage covered by homeowners insurance?

According to the Massachusetts Division of Insurance, standard homeowners policies do not cover flood damage. A separate flood policy is needed, and a sump pump or water backup endorsement may add limited coverage for some backups.

How long before a flood policy takes effect?

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says a National Flood Insurance Program policy usually has a 30-day waiting period. A policy bought after storm warnings will usually not cover that storm.

Should I start drying before the adjuster comes?

Generally yes. Insurers expect reasonable steps to limit damage, and standing water or wet drywall keeps causing harm each day. Photograph everything first and keep receipts.

Ask your insurer if you are unsure what they need to see before demolition.

Will a restoration company handle the claim for me?

No. A restoration company dries the structure and provides documentation, but you file the claim and work with your insurer. No one can promise a claim outcome.

Does Holliston have many flood insurance policies in force?

The 2024 Hazard Mitigation Plan counted 54 National Flood Insurance Program policies in force when it was written, in a town where flood areas cover about 1.78 percent of the land area.

If your address is near a mapped brook or Lake Winthrop, ask an agent about flood coverage rather than assuming your homeowners policy includes it.
