Wells, septic and the way Sherborn homes work

Sherborn has no public water and no public sewer. According to the town's hazard mitigation plan, residents rely on private wells and septic systems, and the entire town is an aquifer recharge area. Most homes sit on lots of one to three acres, and more than half of the town is open space.

That changes how water emergencies play out. There is no town main with a shutoff at the street. A burst pipe means finding the main valve where the line enters the house or at the pressure tank, and a power outage can stop the well pump and the sump pump at the same time.

Where Sherborn floods

The plan describes 1 percent annual-chance floodplains along the Charles River, Sewall Brook, Course Brook, Doppinger and Dirty Meadow Brooks, Leland Mill Pond and Bogastow Brook, which also runs through Holliston. Locally flooded spots include Farm Road at the Charles River bridge, Coolidge Street, Lake Street, Western Avenue and Nason Hill Road.

It names flood years in 1978, 1979, 1987, 1991, 1996, 1998, 2001, 2004, 2006, 2007 and 2010, and one repetitive-loss home flooded in 2010 and again in 2014. Routes 16 and 27 stream crossings are also flood-vulnerable, which is worth knowing if your route to the house crosses water.

When water is spreading in Sherborn

Call (508) 446-9244 day or night. The dispatcher will ask where the water came from, whether you are on a private well and what you have already shut off.

If a septic tank or drain field is part of the problem, the Sherborn Board of Health is the right authority for the system itself. We handle the water damage inside the house, and we tell you honestly when the cause sits outside it.

Sherborn at a glance

Water

Private wells serve the town. The state and the town do not monitor them, so owners test their own water. A 2023 program tested 41 residential wells.

Sewer

Septic systems serve homes, and the Board of Health is the local authority for them.

Permits

The Building Department handles repair permits, and the Board of Health handles wells and septic.

Dispatch

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock for every Sherborn address.

Historic center, open land and old buildings

Sherborn was settled in 1652 and incorporated in 1674. Sherborn Center has had a local historic district since 1983, and the town has National Register districts as well. About a fifth of its 1,539 housing units were built before 1940.

Older houses on large lots often have long supply runs from a pressure tank, and any leak under a slab or in a long run can go unnoticed. A jump in your well pump runtime is a good clue to check for a hidden leak.

Sources: Sherborn Hazard Mitigation Plan (2019), Sherborn town pages on private wells and historic districts, Sherborn 2023 Annual Town Report. Ask the Building Department about permit rules.

If water is still spreading in Sherborn

Find the main

Look for the main valve where the line enters the house or at the pressure tank. Turn off the pump if you can do so safely.

Mind the outage

If the power is out, a sump pump is not running. Lift valuables off the floor and avoid wading through water.

Stay out of live circuits

If water is near the panel or outlets, call your utility or 911 first.
